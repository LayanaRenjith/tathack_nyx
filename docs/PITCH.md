# Problem statement and pitch

## Problem statement

UPI has become how India pays, but its one safety step, "verify the payee's name before you pay", assumes the user can see, read English, hear and tap precisely. Blind users can't see the payee name or spot a fake QR sticker. Elderly users confuse ₹500 with ₹5000. People with tremors mistype amounts. Deaf shopkeepers can't hear the soundbox. Rural, Malayalam-only users can't read English names. So they hand their phone and trust to others, which is exactly what QR-swap and "scan to receive money" scams exploit (UPI fraud cases rose 85% in FY 2024).

## Solution

SafeScan is an accessibility and safety layer in front of any UPI app. It reads the payee from the QR, matches it against the shop the user names, flags scams and confirms the amount by voice, vibration and big text in Malayalam, then hands off to the user's own UPI app for the PIN.

## Track 5 fit

| Group | SafeScan |
|---|---|
| Persons with disabilities | Blind (voice, vibration guidance, fake QR check), deaf (soundbox captions), motor (tremor keypad) |
| Elderly users | Big, simple flow, amount in words, extra-zero check |
| Rural communities | Voice-first, offline safety checks, runs on low-end phones |
| Non-English speakers | Malayalam throughout |

## Pitch lines

- "Every UPI safety tip says 'check the name.' Millions of Indians can't. SafeScan checks it for them."
- "Hello! UPI lets you pay by voice. Nothing lets a blind person check who they're paying."
- "We don't replace UPI apps. We make every one of them accessible, today."
